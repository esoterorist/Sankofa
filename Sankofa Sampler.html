Tap the wave to select and hear a slice. Double-tap to drop a marker. Drag a numbered tab to move its marker. Tap-chop plays the sample and drops a marker every time you hit T or the button, the way you chop on an MPC.

01—

Double-tap any fader label to reset it. Pitch here changes speed too, like a classic sampler; for pitch without speed, use Warp → Texture.

Source tempo comes from the scan; fix it there if it reads double or half. Print bakes the warp and converter into a new sample, keeping your markers.

The converter re-samples the whole sample at a lower rate and bit depth, the way the old machines did when you sampled into them. Pitching a slice afterwards keeps the grit, so pitch down for that dusty swing.

Color runs live on everything you play and is printed into exports when Print FX is on. Wow and flutter add tape drift; vinyl adds hiss and crackle under the beat.

Resample takes

Every export arrives as a .zip of WAV files named for your sample; All slices also includes a slice map with start and end times. Resample records whatever you play, effects and all, into a take you can load back in as a new sample.

Sankofa SKF—16 sample machine. Sankofa is an Akan word and symbol: go back and fetch it. Built for underground electronic music: chop, stretch, crush, resample. Slicing after the transient, beat and region modes of modern DAW samplers; warp after time-stretch engines; converter models after 12-bit drum samplers; color after groovebox effect buses.
